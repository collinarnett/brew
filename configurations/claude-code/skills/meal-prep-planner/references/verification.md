## Search first, always

Search for the actual value of every protein, grain, legume, and branded item. Memory-derived nutrition figures are wrong at a rate that matters across 10 servings.

**Search patterns that work:**
- `[cut] nutrition per 4 oz raw protein calories USDA`
- `[brand] [product] nutrition facts label`
- `[canned legume] nutrition per half cup fiber`

**Prefer:** USDA FoodData Central, manufacturer sites, retailer product pages showing the actual label panel. Retailer pages are often best for branded items since they reproduce the panel.

**Use raw weights for meat.** That's how it's sold and portioned. State "8 oz raw" explicitly so nobody double-counts shrinkage.

**When sources conflict, say so and pick the middle.** Chicken thigh data legitimately ranges from ~115 to ~180 cal per 100g depending on trim and whether the product is enhanced. Tell the person their package label beats any database.

## Yields and conversions

Needed whenever a shopping list or prep step names one form and a recipe names another. Approximate; brands and cooking method vary.

**Dry grain → cooked volume**

| Grain | 1 cup dry yields |
|---|---|
| White or brown rice | ~3 cups |
| Bulgur | ~3 cups |
| Quinoa | ~3 cups |
| Farro | ~2½ cups |
| Pearl barley | ~3½ cups |
| Dry pasta | ~2–2½ cups |
| Dry lentils/beans | ~2½ cups |

**Cans and packages**

| Item | Yields |
|---|---|
| 15 oz can legumes, drained | ~1½ cups |
| 14.5 oz can tomatoes | ~1¾ cups |

**Raw → cooked, by weight**

| Item | Retains |
|---|---|
| Ground meat, browned and drained | ~70–75% |
| Roasted or seared whole cuts | ~75% |
| Leafy greens, wilted | ~25% by volume |

**Produce, whole → prepared**

| Item | Yields |
|---|---|
| Medium onion | ~1 cup diced |
| Large head cauliflower/broccoli | ~6–8 cups florets |
| Small cabbage head | ~8 cups shredded |
| Bunch of kale, stemmed | ~6–8 cups torn |
| Medium cucumber | ~1½ cups sliced |
| Medium lemon | ~3 tbsp juice |
| Medium lime | ~2 tbsp juice |

The lime and lemon figures matter more than they look: recipes calling for "juice of 2 limes" alongside a list saying "2 limes" leave no margin, and juice yield varies a lot with fruit size and freshness.

## Time and equipment anchors

Rough figures for sanity-checking a timeline. Vary by knife skills, kitchen layout, and how much is pre-cut.

**Prep tasks** — the part usually left out of estimates:

| Task | Rough time |
|---|---|
| Washing and cutting a bunch of greens | 8–10 min |
| Cleaning and halving leeks | 6 min |
| Dicing an onion and a few peppers | 8 min |
| Breaking down a head of cauliflower | 6 min |
| Washing a pan mid-session | 3–4 min |
| Portioning ~10 servings into containers | 12–15 min |

**Equipment capacity** — these are physical limits, worth checking against scaled quantities:

| Constraint | Rough limit | Past it |
|---|---|---|
| Sheet pan, protein in one layer | ~1.5–2 lb | Steams instead of roasting |
| 12" skillet, ground meat | ~1.5 lb | No browning |
| Standard oven | 2 pans comfortably | Rotation and ~20% more time |
| Fridge shelf | servings × meals + sauce jars | More containers than expected |

**Vessel count** correlates with whether a session gets finished. Somewhere around five, washing mid-session becomes unavoidable and adds real time; past that the sink tends to become the bottleneck. Worth counting explicitly and confirming against what the person said they'd tolerate.

**Scaling** is linear for proteins, grains, and vegetables, and sub-linear for aromatics — herbs, garlic, citrus, and ginger rarely need doubling when servings double.

## Pre-prepared ingredient equivalents

Default to these forms. Nutrition is essentially unchanged; the time saved is most of prep day.

| Fresh form | Buy instead | Time saved | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Head of cauliflower/broccoli | Bagged florets | 6–8 min | None |
| Cabbage head | Bagged shredded slaw | 5 min | Can't be cut into wedges |
| Whole onion | Pre-diced, fresh or frozen | 5–8 min | Frozen won't caramelize as well |
| Carrots | Matchstick or shredded | 5 min | None |
| Bunch of kale/greens | Bagged pre-washed | 8–10 min | None |
| Garlic cloves | Jarred minced (½ tsp = 1 clove) | 3–5 min | Acid-packed; reads flat raw |
| Fresh ginger | Ginger paste (1 tsp = 1 tbsp grated) | 3 min | None |
| Dried legumes | Canned, rinsed | 40+ min | Rinsing removes ~40% sodium |
| Dry grains | 90-second pouches | 15–25 min | Costs more per serving |
| Whole spices | Pre-ground | 2 min | Slightly less aromatic |

**Frozen vegetables** are equal to fresh nutritionally and often better than out-of-season fresh. They roast well straight from frozen — never thaw first, and don't crowd the pan. They cannot be used raw, and shredded frozen vegetables can't hold a wedge or steak shape.

**Product forms that are not interchangeable.** These get substituted in the aisle and surface as problems mid-cook: salsa verde (a cooking base with pulp) versus taco sauce (a thin condiment); crushed versus diced tomatoes; stock versus broth; mild body chiles like poblano or Anaheim versus hot chiles like serrano or jalapeño, which are 10–25x hotter and cannot be swapped by count. When a recipe depends on the form, say which and why.

Swapping regular soy for low-sodium is usually the single largest sodium reduction available, at no cost to flavor.

## Recurring errors

Check each of these before publishing.

**Uncounted oil.** One tablespoon is 120 calories. It hides in three places: the marinade (count ~60%, the rest stays in the bowl), the roasting pan, and the skillet. Forgetting one vegetable pan is a 60-calorie error.

**Oil-based sauces.** Chimichurri, romesco, pesto, and vinaigrettes run 120–150 cal per serving, nearly all from oil. Always list them separately so the person can spoon rather than pour.

**Forgotten starches.** Noodles and pasta get overlooked when they sit under a protein that dominates the dish description.

**Cut-dependent proteins.** Never average across cuts. Ribeye and sirloin differ by ~270 calories per 8 oz.

**Portion drift when scaling.** Scaling up means more servings, not larger ones. Check the scaled total against pan and skillet capacity before writing the timeline.

**Aromatics don't scale linearly.** Doubling a recipe rarely means doubling garlic, herbs, citrus, or ginger. One bunch of parsley makes plenty of chimichurri for four servings.

## Branded products

These need label lookups, not estimates.

**Enhanced/broth-injected meat.** Frozen store-brand chicken is often injected with broth and salt. It runs ~16g protein per 4 oz instead of ~22g, and ~220mg sodium instead of ~105mg. Tell the difference from the ingredient list: if anything but the meat is listed, it's enhanced. Fresh meat-case product usually isn't. This one substitution can move a meal's protein by 12g.

**Instant oatmeal.** Regular flavored packets run ~160 cal / 4g protein. The "protein" line runs ~220 cal / 10–12g. Lower-sugar runs ~120 cal / 4g. Ask which.

**Protein powder.** Most whey blends are ~120 cal and 24g protein per scoop, but sodium is ~130mg per scoop, which adds up at two scoops.

**"Lite" dressings.** 45–90 cal and 200–400mg sodium per 2 tbsp. Often a bigger sodium contributor than anything else in a salad.

**Premium ice cream.** Runs 230–280 cal per serving versus ~140 for standard brands — the higher butterfat is the point of the product.

**Snack bags.** Portion-size bags are usually 70 cal; the same product measured at 1 oz is ~130. Check which the person means.

## Daily reference points

For context when presenting totals. Give these as reference points, not prescriptions.

- **Sodium:** 2,300mg/day is the general cap; ~1,500mg is a tighter target for blood-pressure concerns.
- **Fiber:** 28g is the Daily Value.
- **Protein:** commonly cited targets run 0.7–1.0g per pound of body weight for people training with resistance.

If someone states a target in units that don't quite parse — "per pound of body fat," for instance — note it lightly, say what you think they meant, and compute both if it's ambiguous. Don't silently substitute your own interpretation.

**Present the day, not just the dinner.** A table showing baseline + dinner against each target is the most useful single artifact in the plan, because it shows at a glance which gaps a dinner change can close and which have to be addressed elsewhere in the day.
